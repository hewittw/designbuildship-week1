Season Preview · GLLL Division 2

Eleven and Oh. Now comes the hard part.

UChicago Men’s Club Lacrosse ran the table in 2024 and carried the Great Lakes Lacrosse League’s Division 2 trophy home from Madison. The spring 2027 roster is still unwritten — and every UChicago student, of any year or division, is invited to help write it.

By The MLAX Board Chicago, Ill. October 2026 12 min read
Sign up for spring → Read the preview

There is a particular kind of quiet that settles over a team after an undefeated season. Nobody says the number out loud. Eleven games, eleven wins, and at the end of it a trophy taller than a midfielder’s stick, handed over at the Great Lakes Lacrosse League’s Division 2 championship in Madison, Wisconsin. UChicago Men’s Club Lacrosse — MLAX, if you are reading the RSO directory — finished 2024 as the best D2 club program in a league that stretches across seven states.

That banner still hangs over everything the program does. It is on the recruiting flyers and in the group chat, and it is the first thing a new player hears about at the first fall practice. But the people who run this club will tell you that the trophy is the least interesting thing about it. The interesting thing is how a team with no cuts, no scholarships and no athletic department behind it got there — and what it intends to do next.

The answer, as best anyone can put it into words, comes down to three of them.

Part I · The Identity

Fun, Camaraderie, Grit

The club’s three stated values, and what each one looks like on a Tuesday night in February.

i.

FunWhy anyone shows up at all

Club sports at a school like this one compete with problem sets, lab hours, internships and thesis drafts for every minute of a student’s week. Nobody is required to be here. So the first value is the simplest and the most honest: practice should be the best ninety minutes of your day. Wall ball turns into a contest; drills end in a scrimmage; road trips to Wisconsin turn into the stories people tell at graduation.

It also means beginners are genuinely welcome. If you have never cradled a ball, you will be taught — the club’s own registration states its purpose as to play and teach the game of lacrosse at a collegiate level, and the word teach is doing real work in that sentence.

ii.

CamaraderieThe reason people stay

Membership is open to any UChicago student, undergraduate or graduate, in any major or division, consistent with the University’s non-discrimination policy. In practice that produces a locker room that looks like nowhere else on campus: a College first-year next to a law student next to someone finishing a doctorate, all in the same maroon CHICAGO jersey.

The van rides, the shared hotel rooms in Madison, the team dinners after a home game — those are where a roster becomes a team. The championship photo on this page has a player wearing a T-shirt over his pads that reads If you’re not first, you’re last. It is a joke. It is also, that particular spring, a fairly accurate scouting report.

iii.

GritHow 11–0 actually happens

Eleven straight wins are not an accident. They are the residue of winter conditioning sessions nobody posted about, of ground balls fought for in the fourth quarter of a game already in hand, of a defense that refused to give away a cheap goal. Club teams do not have a coaching staff to impose that standard. Players hold one another to it, which is harder, and which is the point.

Part II · The Landscape

Inside the Great Lakes Lacrosse League

Forty-odd programs, seven states, one trophy per division.

The Great Lakes Lacrosse League is the regional home of non-varsity men’s club field lacrosse across Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Michigan, Minnesota, the Dakotas and Wisconsin — roughly forty-plus teams in all. It is where UChicago plays: the team’s own description is a club playing other top club lacrosse programs in the North West region through the Great Lakes Lacrosse League.

The league’s founding and notable members read like a map of the Midwest’s lacrosse culture: Notre Dame, Loyola University Chicago, Carleton College, St. Norbert College, the University of Wisconsin, and the Wisconsin system schools at La Crosse, Platteville and Whitewater, plus Mad Cow Lacrosse Club, a Milwaukee-based adult and alumni side that brings a different kind of experience to the field. UChicago competes in Division 2, and every spring the divisions converge on Madison for the league tournament.

Primer · How the season works

Fall (now): the off-season. Recruiting, conditioning, sign-ups, and the league meeting where the whole GLLL coordinates logistics, officiating and scheduling.

Spring: the season proper, roughly March through April, with home games on UChicago’s turf by Stagg Field and road trips to opponent campuses.

Mid-April: the GLLL tournament in Madison, Wisconsin. That is where 2024 ended with a trophy.

Part III · The Preview

Spring 2027: what to watch for

A matchup-by-matchup look at the season ahead. Dates are the club’s working schedule and may shift once the league finalizes it.

Defending a standard is a different job from setting one. Opponents who circled UChicago on the calendar after 2024 will do so again, and a roster that turns over every year has to relearn the habits that produced eleven wins. Here is the road, in order — beginning, as every GLLL season does, not on a field but around a table.

Part IV · The Masthead

Who runs the show

MLAX is entirely student-run, led by a small board of captains and officers.

There is no head coach’s office to call and no athletic department to email. Schedules, travel, budgets and league paperwork are handled by students who also have to make it to class. If you have a question about joining, the president is the person to ask — directly.
